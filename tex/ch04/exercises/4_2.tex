If we take the log of (4.17), we have the following:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \log{\bigl(p_k(x)\bigr)} &= \log{\left(\frac{\pi_k \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}
          \exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_k)}^2\right)}}
          {\sum_{l=1}^K{\pi_l \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}
    \exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_l)}^2\right)}}}\right)} \\
    &= \log{\left(\pi_k \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}
    \exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_k)}^2\right)}\right)} \\
    &\quad - \log{\left(\sum_{l=1}^K{\pi_l \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}
    \exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_l)}^2\right)}}\right)}.
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Now, the second term does not vary with our choice of class $k$. Thus we are
looking to assign the observation to the class for which the following is
largest:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    & \log{\left(\pi_k \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}
    \exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_k)}^2\right)}\right)} \\
    &= \log{(\pi_k)} + \log{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}
    \exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_k)}^2\right)}\right)} \\
    &= \log{(\pi_k)} + \log{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma^2}\right)}
    - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_k)}^2.
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Again, the middle term in the above does not vary with our choice of class
$k$, so that we are looking to maximize the following:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \log{(\pi_k)} - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}{(x - \mu_k)}^2 &= \log{(\pi_k)}
    - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}(x^2 - 2 x \cdot \mu_k + \mu_k^2) \\
    &= x \cdot \frac{\mu_k}{\sigma^2} - \frac{\mu_k^2}{2\sigma^2}
    - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}x^2 + \log{(\pi_k)}.
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Finally, we may neglect the $x^2$ term since it also does not depend upon $k$
and we conclude that by maximizing (4.17), we are also looking to maximize
\[
  x \cdot \frac{\mu_k}{\sigma^2} - \frac{\mu_k^2}{2\sigma^2} + \log{(\pi_k)}
  = \delta_k(x).
\]
